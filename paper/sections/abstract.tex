\begin{abstract}
Few-shot scientific curve models infer entity state from sparse measurements,
but raw latent coordinates are chart-dependent: an invertible
reparameterization can preserve every prediction while changing the coordinates
given to symbolic regression.  We instead project responses on fixed physical
probes onto a named basis fixed or selected on development entities and sealed
before external evaluation.  The resulting coordinates are canonical only
within that specification; an unseen entity's coefficients then use support
targets alone.  This construction is invariant to response-equivalent
representations, and stable response-metric Gauss--Newton preserves independently
recalibrated predictions to $3.66\times10^{-10}$ under controlled affine chart
interventions.  In single-use future cohorts, compact three-coordinate equations
reach physical $R^2=0.9888$ on 30 thermoelectric materials and $R^2=0.9996$ on
84 vapor-pressure compounds.  Their terms describe reference level, sensitivity,
and curvature or effective thermodynamic correction.  Yet kNN is not
significantly worse for thermoelectrics, and log-pressure PCHIP is more accurate.
Our contribution is therefore a basis-relative inference protocol---not a new
latent predictor or universal canonicalizer---with expression fidelity distinct
from prediction and causal recovery.
\end{abstract}
